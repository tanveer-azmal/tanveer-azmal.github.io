Story

The story so far

Azmol trained as a scholar of Bangla language and literature at Khulna University — a BA (Honours) and MA spent close-reading the grammar, sound, and shape of his own language. That grounding turned out to be rare fuel for a field that didn't quite exist yet: teaching that same language to machines.

His first instincts for structured, ground-level work came early — leading field research and enumeration teams for telecom market studies, then supervising volunteer campaigns at Jago Foundation. In 2019 he stepped into govtech as a Senior Associate Linguist at REVE Systems, contributing to EBLICT, the Government of Bangladesh's national programme (Bangladesh Computer Council, ICT Division) to bring Bangla into OCR, speech recognition, and text-to-speech — coordinating a linguistics team against government delivery milestones.

Since 2022, as Lead Linguist of the Bengali.AI research community, he's moved from executing a specification to writing one: he designed the grapheme decomposition and orthographic annotation schema — root graphemes, vowel and consonant diacritics — that now underpins Bengali handwritten-character recognition datasets, and set the phonetic, morphological, and spelling guidelines adopted across Bengali speech and text corpora.

The throughline hasn't changed: make Bangla legible to machines — in handwriting, speech, and sign — without losing what makes it Bangla.

Alongside that, from October 2023 to May 2024, he took on a parallel academic appointment as a Research Assistant at United International University, as the focal researcher on a project to build a Bengali document layout-analysis system for the newspaper domain. He digitized and led the annotation of over 100,000 newspaper pages spanning different eras and print conditions, directing a team of 20 annotators to produce what stands as the largest Bengali newspaper layout-analysis dataset of its kind.

That academic rigor has carried into print: he's a published co-author on Bengali.AI's regional-dialect speech corpora — Ben-10 and its successor RegSpeech12 — and on the first large-scale framework for Bangla IPA transcription. Together, that work is starting to shape how Bengali dialects and phonetics get modeled computationally, in the literature as well as in production systems.

In 2025, the scope came full circle: as a Linguistics Researcher at Genweb2, he returned to EBLICT — this time extending it into Bangladesh Sign Language (BdSL) digitization, the third major modality, after handwriting and speech, that he's helped teach a machine to understand.

Off the clock, Azmol also writes — scriptwriting and direction sit alongside the annotation schemas as a way of shaping how a story gets told, whether the audience is a person or a model.

Research

Publications

Peer-reviewed and preprint work co-authored alongside Bengali.AI colleagues, focused on Bengali dialects, phonetics, and the datasets that language-AI systems are trained on.

  • RegSpeech12: A Regional Corpus of Bengali Spontaneous Speech Across Dialects

    2025 · Extended regional coverage to twelve Bengali dialect regions with over 100 hours of spontaneous speech — one of the few open speech resources documenting Bangla's dialectal diversity.

  • Are ASR Foundation Models Generalized Enough to Capture Features of Regional Dialects for Low-Resource Languages?

    IJCNLP-AACL 2025 · Built Ben-10, the first and largest annotated ASR dataset for ten regional Bengali dialects, and showed that state-of-the-art speech models struggle badly outside standard colloquial Bengali.

  • IPA Transcription of Bengali Texts

    2024 · Proposed a consistent framework for Bangla IPA transcription and introduced DUAL-IPA, the first large-scale sentence-level IPA dataset for Bengali (150k sentences), resolving decades of disagreement among Bangla phoneticians.
